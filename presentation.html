Mapping Renewable Energy Siting
in Italy

A Machine Learning Presence-Background Approach

Alice Di Bella · CMCC Foundation

Solar PV Wind Species Distribution Models Random Forest Gradient Boosting Regional Heterogeneity

Motivation

Italy's Energy Transition

  • EU target: 42.5% renewable energy by 2030
  • Italy needs massive solar & wind deployment
  • Competing land-use pressures: agriculture, nature, heritage

The Siting Challenge

  • Where do plants get built, and why?
  • Which environmental, social & infrastructural factors drive location choices?
  • Do drivers vary across Italian macro-regions?

Our Approach

  • SDM-inspired presence-background modelling
  • Treat existing plant locations as "presences"
  • Randomly sample background sites as pseudo-absences
  • Fit four ML classifiers, compare performance & drivers
  • Predict suitability across all suitable areas in Italy

Key question: Can we learn the spatial fingerprint of past siting decisions to predict future high-probability locations and reveal the trade-offs involved?

Pseudo-Absence Design

Absence of a plant does not mean the site is unsuitable — background sampling must be done carefully.

1

Stratified sampling within NUTS-2 regions to ensure geographic coverage

2

10 independent background groups generated via repeated random draws

3

Buffer exclusion around known presence locations (5 km for solar)

4

Each background group → one full model fit (GLM, Lasso, RF, GBM)

5

Model selection: group with median AIC (5th lowest) used for final inference

Why median-AIC? Avoids selecting the best-fitting group by chance; the median group represents a typical, not exceptional, background realisation.

Sample sizes (median group)

  • Solar: ~2,500 presence + ~12,000 background
  • Wind: ~1,200 presence + ~6,000 background

Covariate extraction

  • All predictors extracted at plant centroid (presence) or background point
  • Raster resolution: ~1 km (ETRS89 LAEA)
  • Zonal aggregation for polygon presences

Covariates

Infrastructure

  • tx — Distance to transmission lines (km)
  • roads — Distance to major roads (km)
  • sub — Distance to substations (km)

Topography & Environment

  • slope — Terrain slope (°)
  • env — Environmental protection score
  • cf — Wind capacity factor (wind only)

Socioeconomic

  • pop — Population density (inh/km²)
  • unemploy — Unemployment rate (%)
  • poverty — At-risk-of-poverty rate (%)
  • gdp_pc — GDP per capita (€)
  • inst_quality — Institutional quality index
  • lag — Spatial lag of wind plants (wind only)

Land Use / Land Cover

  • 8 binary LULC classes (Corine Land Cover)
  • Forest, Grassland, Shrubland, Riparian
  • Vegetated, Agriculture, Developed, Other
  • + 21 NUTS-2 region fixed effects

All continuous predictors standardised (mean=0, SD=1) prior to modelling. LULC dummies are excluded from logistic coefficient plots due to quasi-complete separation but are included in tree-based models.

Modelling Framework

Four Models

  • GLM — Logistic regression (benchmark)
  • Lasso — L1-penalised logistic (glmnet); λ via 10-fold CV
  • RF — Random Forest (caret + ranger); 500 trees, mtry tuned
  • GBM — Gradient Boosting (caret + xgboost); shrinkage + depth tuned

Cross-Validation

  • Standard 10-fold CV: overall discrimination (AUC-ROC)
  • Spatial block CV: tests for spatial overfitting; blocks defined by clustering presence locations into geographic groups

Output

  • Predicted suitability maps (0–1 probability) for all 8 model × technology combinations
  • Variable importance (RF/GBM: permutation importance; GLM/Lasso: standardised coefficients)
  • Regional effects via GLMM with NUTS-2 random slopes

Spatial block CV reveals whether models generalise across space or merely interpolate within the training range — critical for policy use.

Key Drivers

Solar PV

Positive drivers (increase probability)

  • Proximity to substations & transmission lines
  • Flat terrain (low slope)
  • Agricultural land cover
  • Southern regions (higher irradiance)

Negative drivers (reduce probability)

  • High population density
  • Protected natural areas (env score)
  • Forested land cover

Wind

Positive drivers

  • High wind capacity factor
  • Existing wind clusters (spatial lag)
  • Proximity to transmission infrastructure
  • Ridgeline / elevated terrain

Negative drivers

  • Environmental protection score
  • Dense population areas
  • Proximity to major roads (access paradox)

Method

  • Mixed-effects logistic regression (lme4)
  • 21 NUTS-2 regions as grouping factor
  • Random slopes for key predictors
  • Fallback: stratified per-region GLM if GLMM singular

Findings

  • Population density effect varies strongly: more negative in densely populated North
  • Transmission distance effect consistent across regions
  • Environmental score more constraining in Centre-North
  • Wind spatial lag stronger in South & Islands (clustering)

Conclusions

🗺

Siting is predictable. ML models achieve AUC > 0.78 (solar) and > 0.89 (wind), confirming that location choices follow learnable spatial patterns driven by infrastructure access, topography, and land cover.

⚡

Infrastructure is the dominant driver. Distance to transmission lines and substations consistently rank as top predictors — policy instruments that reduce grid connection costs could unlock new suitable areas.

🌍

North–South divide persists. Regional heterogeneity analysis reveals that socioeconomic constraints (population density, poverty) operate differently across Italian macro-regions, with the South showing stronger wind clustering effects.

🌱

Environmental-energy trade-offs are real. The environmental protection score consistently reduces siting probability, suggesting ongoing tension between Italy's conservation commitments and renewable expansion targets.

🔮

Next steps: Integrate socioeconomic deprivation indices (ISTAT), project suitability under future land-use scenarios, and assess distributional equity of predicted high-suitability areas.
